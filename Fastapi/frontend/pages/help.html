QUICK START

Start here

  1. 1

    Choose an agent, then a product

    On Shop, choose who is buying at the top of the page, then pick any of the twelve products. Browsing only prepares the product; press Continue with RAMIFY to start the purchase check. Try the Autonomous buying agent with Apex Nutrients Magnesium Glycinate: every check passes, so it completes the simulated purchase itself.

  2. 2

    Read the product result and the agent response

    The product's own posture comes first, then what your agent may do with it. When they differ, the reason is written between them.

  3. 3

    Use the permitted action

    Consumer-style agents stage a receipt-backed basket line. Procurement-style agents create a signed simulated requisition instead. The signed receipt controls which path is available.

  4. 4

    Try a case that needs attention

    Same agent, now Greenline Botanicals Ashwagandha: its lab certificate lapsed 90 days before the snapshot date, and the agent stops and asks.

  5. 5

    Read the receipt

    Your receipt shows the product, reference, when it was sealed, who it was checked for and the decision in plain English. Open What was found or Technical record for the rest.

  6. 6

    Answer it

    On Needs me, press Why it needs you, Compare alternatives if you would rather buy something else, then decline or authorise once. Both are recorded.

  7. 7

    Verify the history

    On Activity, press Verify receipt history.

FAQ

Questions people ask

Am I actually buying anything?

No. Nothing here touches a real shop, a real payment or real stock. The products, brands, sellers, laboratories and prices are invented. It is a working model of how the checking would go, not a shop.

What are the seven checks?

Whether the product could be identified at all; whether there is a recall or advisory against the batch; whether the seller is established for this category; whether the paperwork the category requires is attached; whether that paperwork is still in date; whether the claims come from bodies entitled to make them; and whether any two sources contradict each other.

Each returns pass, review, fail or incomplete. All seven are shown under What was checked on any result.

Why is "incomplete" not just a fail?

Because missing evidence is not evidence of a problem. If a certificate was never attached, that tells you nothing about the product, only about the filing. Treating the two the same would either condemn sound products or teach people to ignore real failures.

Why did my agent stop on something that looks fine?

Because "fine" is two different questions. The product may satisfy every check and still be something your agent is not allowed to buy: over your spending limit, from a brand you have not approved, or from a seller you have no arrangement with. The result names your rule rather than the product.

My agent stopped and I still want the thing. What now?

Compare alternatives looks for something the same agent could actually buy; every candidate is fully assessed for that agent first. Or choose Authorise once, which records your decision in a separate system-sealed Human Receipt. Alternatives are never offered against a recall.

Can I change what my agent is allowed to do?

Yes. On My agents, press Edit what it may do to change its spending ceiling, approved brands and sellers, whether warnings come to you, and whether it may buy without asking. New agent builds one from scratch, with a live preview of what it could then do.

Could I set up an agent that buys something unsafe?

No. There is no setting for it. A recalled product permits one action, stop, for every agent, including any you invent. Your settings can only make an outcome stricter, and that is enforced in the code.

What is a receipt for?

Proving, later, what was checked, what was found, what was decided and when. It is sealed so any change afterwards shows up. Press Check this receipt to verify one, or Test a changed copy to watch a tampered copy get rejected.

What happens if I authorise a stopped item?

A separate Human Receipt records the decision, what RAMIFY found, what the agent did, the note, and the original receipt ID and hash. The original is left exactly as it was. A recall is different: it is blocked and never enters review.

Does the AI decide anything?

No. When local AI is ready, LangGraph sends the shopper's words to a Llama 3.1 model running through Ollama on this machine. The model may identify a known catalogue item and explain a sealed receipt. Every trust judgement, policy decision and purchase permission comes from deterministic RAMIFY code.

How do I know the local LLM is really running?

Check the top-right badge. LOCAL AI · LLAMA3.1 means the live LangGraph and Ollama path is ready. LOCAL · AI FALLBACK means RAMIFY is using the safe deterministic matcher instead.

Does this need the internet?

Only for first-time package and model downloads. The server uses loopback, and the optional model connection goes to the local Ollama service on the same machine.

What are the demo stories?

Seventeen named situations the prototype was built to demonstrate: a recall, advisories, expired and withdrawn certificates, a discontinued product with a named replacement, an unverified seller, and a product with nothing on file. Choosing one prepares its product and agent; RAMIFY still waits for you to continue.

Something looks out of date.

Reload the page. The build reference in the footer changes whenever the interface does; if it does not match what you expect, you are looking at an old copy.
