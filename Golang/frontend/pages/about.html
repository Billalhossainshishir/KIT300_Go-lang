About RAMIFY OS

A prototype for checking product evidence before an agent acts, and for keeping a clear record of what was checked, decided and authorised.

Synthetic demonstration data. Brands, sellers, issuers, certificates, advisories and prices are fictional and certify nothing in the real world. Every action is simulated. Not for real purchasing, safety, legal or compliance decisions.

Shopping is starting to happen without us. You ask for something, software finds it, and increasingly that software can complete the purchase on its own. Which raises a question nobody used to have to ask out loud: how does it know the thing is genuine?

The problem in simple terms

A language model can be useful for understanding a request and explaining a result, but product trust should not depend on generated language alone. A confident explanation is not the same thing as verified evidence.

That is fine when you are asking for a recipe. It is not fine when the product is a batch of examination gloves that was recalled last month, or a supplement whose lab certificate expired in April, or a listing from a seller nobody has ever verified.

What RAMIFY OS does about it

It separates interpretation from product assessment. The model can help understand the request, while deterministic checks evaluate the product facts.

Before your agent buys anything, the product goes through the same seven checks every time. Is this the product it claims to be? Is there a recall or advisory against this batch? Is the seller established for this category? Is the paperwork this category requires attached? Is that paperwork still in date? Do the claims come from bodies entitled to make them? Do any two sources contradict each other?

Those checks read facts and return findings. They give the same answer every time, from the same evidence, no matter who is asking. The AI can help interpret the request and explain the finished result. It is not authorised to create or alter the product-trust decision.

The language model explains the decision. It does not make it.

Two questions, not one

This is the distinction the whole thing is built around, and it is worth a minute.

Is this product sound? That is a question about the product. It has one answer, and everybody gets the same one.

May this buyer act on it? That is a question about you. Your spending limit, your approved brands, your suppliers, whether you want to see anything carrying a warning before it goes through.

Every result on the shop page shows both, side by side, in that order. Your own rules can make an outcome stricter — an agent may always stop on something the checks passed. They cannot make it looser. There is no combination of settings that turns a recall into a purchase, for any agent, including one you build yourself.

What you get back

Three answers, and only three.

Green — a clean approval. The checks are satisfied and no warning is attached.

Orange — attention is required. This can mean an approved result with a warning, or a genuine pause for human review. The label and receipt always say which one; colour alone is never the decision.

Red — something failed on grounds no setting can wave through. Your agent will not proceed, and neither will anybody else's.

Underneath, the system works in five outcomes rather than three, because "allowed, with something you should read" and "allowed" are genuinely different, and so are "stopped on a finding" and "stopped because there was not enough on file to judge". The receipt carries the precise one. The colour is only ever how it is shown to you, and is never stored.

And receipts people can actually read

Every check produces a machine receipt: what was looked at, what was found, what was decided, and when. If a person later decides a reviewable case, the system creates a linked Human Receipt that explains the same record in plain English and keeps the full JSON underneath. Both are sealed at the moment they are written — a SHA-256 hash of the exact bytes, and a system Ed25519 signature over that hash — so if a single character changes later, checking it says so.

Not that the file cannot be edited. Anyone can edit a file. That it cannot be edited without the change being obvious. That is a smaller promise, and it is one that can actually be kept.

So when someone asks you in a year why you bought this, the answer is attached to the purchase. You are not reconstructing it from memory.

Why the design is auditable

Because the product result is separate from the buyer

The product either satisfied the checks or it did not, and everyone gets the same answer to that question. What differs between one buyer and the next is only what they are permitted to do with the answer — and that difference is written into the receipt as its own field, next to the objective one, rather than blended into a single verdict.

Because missing information is shown as missing

Missing evidence is not evidence of a problem, and it is not treated as one. When there is not enough on file to judge something, the answer is that it cannot be judged, and the agent stops. An agent that has never once said "I am not sure" has not been careful, it has been lucky.

Because the receipt can be checked

Every receipt can be verified — by you, by your accountant, by a regulator, on a different computer, months later, without asking us for anything. The button in the interface and the command-line tool shipped with this build run the same code, so they cannot quietly drift apart. A promise you can only take our word for is not worth much.

Because human decisions are recorded separately

When a reviewable case stops, a person may decline it or authorise it once. That choice is recorded in a separate system-sealed Human Receipt next to the original machine receipt, which is left exactly as it was. The ledger only ever grows. Hard stops such as recall do not enter human review and cannot be overridden.

Because agent settings cannot remove hard stops

You can tell your agent to spend more, buy other brands, act without asking. You cannot tell it that a recalled product is fine. The agent editor shows you this directly: whatever you type above, the outcomes that need a person or a stop always read cannot buy.

What this demo includes

A prototype, built for the University of Tasmania's KIT300 capstone for RAMIFY OS. The documented demo configuration binds the application to the local machine; the optional model connection is also directed to local Ollama. This describes the configured architecture rather than claiming to prove network isolation.

It holds twelve products across two categories — nine non-prescription supplements and three items of protective equipment — from five sellers, with twenty evidence records issued by three laboratories and regulators, and seventeen named cases covering a recall, advisories, lapsed and withdrawn certificates, a discontinued product with a named replacement, a seller whose authority is unverified, and one product with nothing on file at all.

Every date is judged against a fixed snapshot of 24 July 2026, so a case that returns a warning today still returns it next year. Five agents come set up; you can edit any of them or build your own.

The honesty boundary

The products, brands, sellers, laboratories, certificates and prices are invented. They describe no real company and certify nothing. Nothing is bought and no money moves.

The cryptography is not invented. Every hash is a genuine SHA-256 digest of the bytes being hashed, and every signature a genuine Ed25519 signature. What is small here is the size of the catalogue, not the honesty of what runs over it.
